# 1. Executive Vision

Kima Consulting is a technology-enabled tax compliance and business advisory company that aims to transform how African businesses and accounting professionals manage tax obligations.

Our mission is to make tax compliance understandable, organized, traceable, and accessible to businesses of all sizes.

Kima will provide a unified platform where taxpayers and accounting professionals can collaborate throughout the entire compliance lifecycle.

The platform will help users:

- Understand applicable tax obligations.
- Register and maintain accurate taxpayer profiles.
- Collect and organize financial documentation.
- Coordinate accounting and declaration preparation.
- Import financial information from existing accounting software.
- Prepare and review tax compliance documentation.
- Monitor deadlines and the status of obligations.
- Preserve evidence of declarations, payments, and correspondence.
- Resolve discrepancies through structured, documented procedures.
- Communicate securely with accountants.
- Access clear explanations of relevant tax regulations.

The platform must be modern, visually compelling, highly intuitive, mobile-friendly, and practical for Cameroonian working conditions.

**Our aspiration is not to build another accounting application. It is to build the trusted operating system for managing tax compliance.**

The product must remain useful even where government filing systems are unavailable, slow, or do not provide authorized external integrations.

# 3. The Problem

Cameroonian businesses and their accountants experience several interconnected challenges.

## 3.1 Difficult document collection

Clients often submit sales invoices, purchase invoices, expense receipts, and bank statements through WhatsApp or as physical documents.

These records may be scattered, incomplete, duplicated, or delivered late.

Accountants spend substantial time requesting, collecting, checking, and organizing information.

## 3.2 Fragmented accounting and tax workflows

A typical observed workflow involves:
1. Receiving taxpayer transactions and supporting documents.
2. Entering financial transactions into Sage.
3. Reviewing accounting records.
4. Exporting accounting information to Excel.
5. Transferring or adapting information to the applicable DSF format.
6. Reviewing the declaration.
7. Submitting through an appropriate DGI service.
8. Keeping records of the outcome.

This process involves manual handling, repeated checks, and potential errors.

## 3.3 Weak document archiving

Some practitioners lack structured systems for preserving historical declarations, financial documents, correspondence, and proof of submission.

Retrieving information from previous years may be difficult.

## 3.4 Poor taxpayer visibility

Clients often do not know:

- Whether their documents were received.
- Which documents are missing.
- Whether work has started.
- Why their declaration is taking time.
- What taxes or fees may be payable.
- Whether the declaration was completed.
- What actions they must take next.

This can lead to repeated telephone calls, frustration, and reduced trust.

## 3.5 Complicated error correction

Accounting errors discovered before submission can generally be reviewed and corrected internally.

Errors identified after a declaration has entered the tax administration's system may require amendment procedures, correspondence, additional supporting documentation, or official assistance.

These processes need proper tracking.

## 3.6 Limited tax literacy

Many taxpayers confuse tax registration, tax declarations, taxable income, tax liability, and tax payment.

Some may avoid formalization because they fear unexpected liabilities.

The opportunity is to provide clear explanations without giving false assurances.

## 3.7 Regulatory change

Tax obligations, filing procedures, accounting standards, deadlines, and government platforms can change.

Accountants need access to current, authoritative information, with previous versions preserved for historical periods.

## Proposed competitive differentiation

**1. Compliance-first design**

Every screen, task, alert, and workflow is organized around a taxpayer's obligations rather than primarily around accounting journals.

**2. Two-sided transparency**

Accountants manage the technical work; taxpayers receive a simplified view of what is happening and why.

**3. Cameroon-specific regulatory intelligence**

Tax guidance is linked to official sources, effective dates, relevant procedures, and reviewed rules.

**4. Document completeness management**

The platform identifies missing documents and makes the responsible party visible.

**5. Explainable Sage-to-DSF preparation**

Financial data imports can be mapped to applicable DSF templates, with traceable calculations and mandatory professional review.

**6. Tax issue and correction management**

Errors, official correspondence, responses, and case histories are organized in one place.

**7. Evidence-based compliance history**

Each completed obligation contains its underlying evidence, review record, and filing documentation.

**8. Practical accessibility**

French/English interfaces, mobile-first document submission, optimized bandwidth, WhatsApp-based notifications where authorized, and local payment options.

The long-term differentiator should be a combination of trustworthy execution, excellent usability, regulatory expertise, and superior customer experience.

## 7.3 Kima DSF Bridge

A specialized workflow connecting imported accounting information to supported DSF preparation processes.

Initial features:

- Import common Sage-generated Excel and CSV exports.
- Detect and normalize spreadsheet columns.
- Let accountants manually resolve ambiguous account mappings.
- Preserve original source files.
- Map verified account balances into supported DSF fields.
- Display the origin and calculation of mapped values.
- Identify missing schedules and unsupported balances.
- Run deterministic validation checks.
- Support accountant overrides with reasons and audit trails.
- Produce downloadable preparation files in a verified applicable format.
- Generate a pre-submission review report.

Important: The relevant **tax regime and applicable DSF accounting format must be modeled separately**. The system normal and the minimum cash-basis accounting system (SMT) are not simply interchangeable labels for the real and simplified tax regimes.

The platform must not claim that all taxpayers must submit a DSF. Applicability must be established under current rules.

## 7.4 Kima Compliance Radar

A personalized compliance planning system.

Features:

- Verified taxpayer obligations.
- Tax calendar.
- Filing preparation windows.
- Reminder schedules.
- Missing-document alerts.
- Deadline risk indicators.
- Tax rule and procedure updates.
- Client-specific announcement targeting.
- Escalation of overdue tasks.

Each rule must have a legal source, jurisdiction, effective date, and verification status.

## 7.5 Kima Clarity AI

A bilingual, AI-assisted tax information and workflow assistant.

Capabilities:

- Explain common tax terminology.
- Help clients understand requested documents.
- Summarize the status of their own declarations.
- Suggest next administrative steps.
- Help accountants draft document requests.
- Help accountants identify possible data inconsistencies.
- Summarize relevant regulatory changes with source references.
- Draft formal correspondence for professional review.
- Explain why a declaration is not yet ready.

Restrictions:

- Never invent legal requirements.
- Never fabricate filing or payment confirmation.
- Never autonomously approve accounting entries or declarations.
- Never modify tax amounts to obtain a preferred outcome.
- Never recommend informal payments or unauthorized intervention.
- Never represent an AI-generated opinion as an official DGI determination.
- Never reveal one taxpayer's private information to another.

When guidance is uncertain or the legal source is outdated, the assistant must say so and escalate to a qualified reviewer.

# 8. Flagship Customer Journey

The platform should provide a continuous experience.

### Stage 1 — Discover Kima

A business owner visits the website and sees:

"Understand your taxes. Organize your records. Stay ready."

They can explore the service, learn how it works, and request professional help.

### Stage 2 — Complete a guided assessment

The user supplies basic business and tax-profile information.

The platform asks understandable questions and flags anything requiring professional validation.

### Stage 3 — Open a compliance workspace

The system creates a private client dashboard with a document checklist and visible next steps.

### Stage 4 — Submit documents

The client uploads files through a mobile phone or computer.

Each document is classified or queued for human review.

### Stage 5 — Accountant performs preparation

An assigned accountant reviews available records, performs accounting work in the appropriate system, imports files when relevant, and prepares the declaration.

### Stage 6 — Quality control

The responsible reviewer checks completeness, calculations, unusual items, and the supporting evidence.

### Stage 7 — Client receives an update

The client sees a simplified status:

"Your records have been reviewed. Your declaration is awaiting final approval."

### Stage 8 — Official submission

An authorized professional or taxpayer performs the required step through the applicable government service.

Where a legally authorized and technically supported integration exists, Kima may offer it.

Otherwise, Kima provides preparation support and captures confirmation evidence.

### Stage 9 — Archiving and continued support

Final documents, evidence, outstanding issues, and next obligations remain in the user's secure archive.

## Mandatory data architecture requirements

- Every firm-controlled operational record must be securely scoped to its tenant.
- Taxpayers can access only their own authorized records.
- A firm must receive explicit authorization before accessing a taxpayer's workspace.
- Taxpayer ownership and firm engagement are separate concepts.
- All material status changes should be auditable.
- Approved declarations should preserve the exact reviewed version.
- Monetary fields must use precise decimal representations or integer minor-unit conventions as appropriate.
- Dates, time zones, accounting periods, and regulatory effective dates must be unambiguous.
- Original imported files must be preserved.
- Source-to-result traceability is mandatory in financial mappings.
- Each country must have separately configurable regulatory rules and document templates.

# 13. Tax Rules and Source Management

Cameroon-specific tax logic must not be encoded as permanent assumptions.

The system must support:

- Applicable jurisdiction.
- Taxpayer category.
- Tax centre.
- Tax regime.
- Accounting framework.
- Applicable obligation.
- Reporting period.
- Filing deadline.
- Effective date.
- Legal reference.
- Source URL or archived legal document.
- Reviewer and approval status.
- Historical versions.

Do not equate a taxpayer's tax regime with the DSF template automatically.

Do not infer a legal filing obligation solely from turnover.

Tax classification must be confirmed against current rules and the taxpayer's official profile.

## Authoritative source hierarchy

1. Relevant legislation and official gazettes.
2. DGI and MINFI regulations, circulars, and official instructions.
3. OHADA accounting standards.
4. Official tax administration technical guides.
5. Reviewed professional commentary for additional explanation.

Tax rules must be subject to qualified professional verification before activation.

# 14. Security and Legal Governance

Financial records and taxpayer information are sensitive.

The product must be designed for confidentiality from the beginning.

Minimum standards:

- Role-based access control.
- Multifactor authentication for privileged users.
- Strong tenant isolation.
- Encryption in transit and at rest.
- Least-privilege access.
- Audit logs for sensitive actions.
- Secure file sharing.
- Consent management.
- Data retention and deletion policies.
- Backup and recovery testing.
- Routine vulnerability testing.
- Incident response procedures.
- Protection against prompt injection and malicious document content.
- Human authorization before external submissions.

Cameroon's personal data protection law must be considered in the product's legal review.

The company must also verify applicable professional authorization, tax representation, record-retention obligations, cross-border data requirements, and permitted government integrations before production deployment.

No government authorization, partnership, or e-filing integration may be represented as established without written verification.

# 18. Critical Product Acceptance Criteria

A development release is not ready until the following scenarios work.

**Scenario A: New taxpayer**

A business owner creates an account, completes onboarding, and securely uploads documents.

The assigned accountant sees the documents and can request missing information.

**Scenario B: Missing documents**

An accountant identifies an absent bank statement.

The client sees the request, receives an appropriate reminder, uploads the statement, and the request is resolved with an audit history.

**Scenario C: Multi-client isolation**

An accountant working for Firm A cannot access Firm B's clients or documents.

A taxpayer cannot access another taxpayer's files through guessed IDs or direct URLs.

**Scenario D: Sage import**

The accountant uploads a supported Sage-generated workbook.

The system preserves the original file, parses the known columns, identifies unmapped data, and does not silently invent or change values.

**Scenario E: Review**

A draft declaration cannot be marked approved without the required checks and an authorized reviewer.

**Scenario F: Submission**

A declaration cannot be represented as officially filed solely because an accountant clicked an internal completion button.

Official submission evidence or a clearly identified authorized integration response must support the recorded filing status.

**Scenario G: Correction**

An accountant opens a correction case, records the reason, attaches correspondence, assigns follow-up, and eventually records a verified resolution.

**Scenario H: Tax guidance**

The assistant answers a supported question with a valid legal source and its applicability date.

Where a source is unavailable or unclear, it does not invent the answer.

**Scenario I: Language**

Core user journeys work fully in French and English.

**Scenario J: Mobile**

A taxpayer can complete onboarding and upload a photographed receipt using a mobile phone and a reasonably slow connection.
